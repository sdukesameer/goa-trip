Five things the calendar decides for you

You arrive in the last week of the monsoon, days before the season opens on 1 October. Most of the plan is unaffected. These are not — and three of them are on the list the group already put together.

Changes the last night

2 October is a statewide dry day

Gandhi Jayanti is Goa's one fixed dry day. Every casino is ordered shut for the full 24 hours and no alcohol is served anywhere — clubs, bars, shacks, shops.

You don't drink, so the ban itself costs you nothing. The catch is second-order: clubs don't open a room they can't run a bar in. So Friday isn't a club night, and the big night moves to Thursday 1 October — which is season-opening night anyway.

Headline club night Fri 2 Oct → Thu 1 Oct, Hammerzz
Cut it

Anjuna flea market won't be up on 30 September

30 Sep 2026 is a Wednesday, so the day is right — but the market is an October-to-April operation, shut through the monsoon. You miss it by one week.

The replacement is better and falls on a day you're already free: Mapusa Friday Market on 2 October — year-round, Friday is its bazaar day, ten minutes from Arpora, and it's where Goans actually shop.

Wed 30 Sep · Anjuna flea → Fri 2 Oct · Mapusa, 8:15 am
Cut it

Dudhsagar is closed, all of it

The Forest Department suspends the Collem jeep safari every monsoon and reopens around mid-to-late October, after your dates. The railway-track walk is banned and patrolled, with fines.

If someone needs a waterfall: Arvalem near Sanquelim is ~35 km from Baga, free, open, and at full flow right now — and it's ten minutes from the Mayem Lake bungee, so the two make one good half-day.

In your favour

Season starts 1 October, mid-trip

Shack licences run 1 Oct to 31 May, so the coast changes character halfway through your week: empty sand and red flags for the first four days, then everything switches on.

That's why this order exists — quiet beaches first (Palolem, Candolim, Sinquerim), switched-on ones after the 1st (Vagator, Morjim, Mandrem).

Cheaper this way

Day 1 no vehicle, Day 2 a van, scooters from Day 3

You arrive tired and Baga is walkable, so nothing is hired on Sunday. Monday's far-south run needs a driver anyway, and the van does your big grocery shop on the way home for free.

Scooters start Tuesday and run to Saturday morning — 4½ days instead of 6½. Same places, roughly ₹4,000 less across the group, and no bikes sitting in a driveway.

It's the Sunday of a shoulder-season week, so call to check they're running and whether a table minimum applies. Walk, don't ride — and you couldn't ride tonight anyway, which is rather the point.

South Goa, in one hired van

Chandor, Cabo de Rama, Agonda, Palolem — the entire south in a single day, done the only way it can safely be done. One 12-seater with a driver, out before the highway wakes up, everyone asleep on the way home.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
6:30 am
Breakfast at the villa, prepped last night
Boil the eggs and set the coffee on Sunday night. Fill flasks, take fruit — you'll want it around 11:00 am.
7:00 am
The van leaves Baga◎— open in Google Maps book ahead
One 12-seater Tempo Traveller with a driver, roughly ₹6,000–8,000 for the day including fuel, tolls and the driver. The early start is the whole trick: NH66 through Panjim and Porvorim clogs after 8:30 am, and every hour you lose in the morning comes straight off Palolem in the evening. Agree the exact stop list and the price in writing the night before — an open-ended 'South Goa sightseeing' booking is how you end up at a spice shop that pays the driver a commission.
9:15 – 10:45 am
Braganza House, Chandor◎— open in Google Maps new phone first
A 350-year-old Portuguese mansion still lived in by the family — ballroom, Belgian chandeliers, a long rosewood gallery, and a relic of St Francis Xavier in the private chapel. Two wings entered separately: about ₹150 a head on one side, a donation box on the other. Open most days, roughly 9:00/10:00 am to 5:00 pm. It's a home, not a museum, and eight unannounced visitors is a lot — ring ahead. Carry cash, there's no card machine. Photography rules differ between the two wings, so ask before you shoot. Mid-morning is when the light rakes down the long gallery, which is exactly when you're there.
12:15 – 1:30 pm
Cabo de Rama Fort◎— open in Google Maps new
Open 9:00 am–5:00 pm daily, free. Laterite ramparts, old cannons, the Chapel of St Anthony, and cliff viewpoints dropping straight into the Arabian Sea. The best photograph of the south leg is the cliff edge past the chapel. In late September the grass is waist-high and violently green, which is the upside. The downside is that the drop is unfenced and wet laterite is slick — stay back from the edge, and nobody walks out to the point alone. 'The Cape' clifftop café next door is seasonal; call before you count on lunch there.
2:00 – 3:15 pm
Lunch and a walk — Agonda Beach◎— open in Google Maps
Two and a half kilometres of near-empty sand, fifteen minutes south of the fort. Calmer than Palolem, a better lunch this early in the season, and the one stop today where nobody needs to be doing anything.
3:45 – 6:45 pm
Palolem Beach◎— open in Google Maps pre-season
The crescent everyone photographs. Walk it end to end, then take the rocks at the south end toward Colomb. Sunset 6:26 pm, light until about 6:48 pm. Set expectations now: hut licences start on 1 October, so on the 28th Palolem is literally being rebuilt — scaffolding, few kitchens open, red flags, no swimming, and no boats to Butterfly or Honeymoon beach. What you get instead is the most famous bay in Goa with almost nobody on it. That's a photograph you cannot take in December.
9:15 pm
Grocery stop — Newton's, Candolim◎— open in Google Maps free ride
Biggest supermarket in North Goa and directly on the road home. Four days of breakfasts, water cans, masalas, snacks — all of it carried in the van instead of on a scooter. Eight people eating breakfast out every day is roughly ₹2,000 a morning and an hour lost each time. A stocked kitchen makes it about ₹400 and gets you on the road by 8:15 am, which is the single thing standing between you and the good light.

Tue 29 Sep

Scooters arrive, then the Latin Quarter and the river

Bikes at the door at 7:30 am, and straight into your longest riding day — Fontainhas, Old Goa, the free ferry to Divar. Tonight is a casino boat, on a Tuesday, which is why it costs about a thousand rupees less.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
7:30 am
4 scooters delivered to the villa◎— open in Google Maps book ahead
Most Baga vendors will drop bikes at your door — ask when you book. Budget ₹400–700 per bike per day. Activa or Jupiter over an NTorq if anyone is a nervous rider. Video all four bikes, all four sides, before money changes hands: scratches, dents, mirrors, tyre tread, fuel gauge, horn, indicators. Count eight helmets, not four — Goa enforces pillion helmets. Photograph each RC and insurance. Never leave an original licence or passport as a deposit; offer a photocopy plus cash.
9:00 – 10:45 am
Fontainhas, Panjim◎— open in Google Maps best light of the week
Asia's largest Latin Quarter: ochre, indigo and oxblood Portuguese houses on non-motorable cobbled lanes. Park once at the Tobacco Square end and walk. Don't miss the Chapel of St. Sebastian — the crucifix inside has Christ's eyes open, because it came from the Palace of the Inquisition — then Gallery Gitanjali and Confeitaria 31 de Janeiro for a Swiss roll and a second coffee. These lanes are narrow enough to act like canyons and they fall into hard shadow by noon. Between 8:30 am and 10:00 am you get soft light bouncing off the pastel walls, no tour groups, and residents who don't mind you yet. This is the most time-sensitive stop of the week — being late costs you the photographs, not the place.
11:05 am
Fabs Bebinca, Ribandar◎— open in Google Maps
Fatima bakes the state's best bebinca — sixteen layers of coconut milk, egg yolk and nutmeg — out of her own heritage home. Not signposted; ask near the Ribandar church. Buy a whole one for the villa.
12:00 pm
Basilica of Bom Jesus + Sé Cathedral◎— open in Google Maps
UNESCO-listed, both free, five minutes apart. St Francis Xavier's remains are in the Basilica; Sé is the largest church in Asia and holds the Golden Bell. Shoulders and knees covered. The Basilica closes to visitors during Mass — check the day's times on arrival and do Sé first if you've walked into one. Both interiors are dim: brace against a pillar rather than pushing ISO, and shoot the Basilica's laterite facade from the lawn on the south side, not head-on.
1:30 pm
Lunch — Viva Panjim or Ritz Classic◎— open in Google Maps
Viva Panjim is a tiny Fontainhas house doing proper Goan home food; Ritz Classic is the fish-thali institution. Either way: prawn balchão, chicken cafreal, kingfish rechado, sol kadhi.
2:45 – 5:15 pm
Divar Island, by free ferry◎— open in Google Maps free crossing
The flat-bottom ferry leaves the Old Goa terminal by the Viceroy's Arch, runs roughly 6:00 am to midnight, and is free for two-wheelers. On the far side: no traffic, no tourists, empty lanes through paddy and palm, and the hilltop church at Piedade looking back over the river. This is the best pure riding of the trip and the reason the scooters are worth having. Ask at the jetty what time the last crossing back runs and don't be on the island without knowing that number — monsoon ferries get suspended in rough water.
7:30 pm
Deltin Royale casino boat, Panjim◎— open in Google Maps new book
India's largest floating casino, boarding at Fisheries Jetty. Tuesday sits in the Monday–Thursday band, so entry starts around ₹2,500–3,000 a head and includes an unlimited buffet plus bonus gaming chips — the ticket is most of your dinner. Live entertainment from 7:00 pm. This is the big night that needs no alcohol at all: it's the buffet, the tables, the show and the boat. Weekends run ₹4,000+, so a weekday is deliberate. Smart casuals, carry photo ID. Take two cabs and leave the bikes at the villa — Panjim to Baga at half past midnight is 16 km and not a ride to end this day on.
Loading route…
₹Scooters start today, not Sunday: 4 bikes × ~₹500 × 4½ days ≈ ₹9,000, about ₹1,125 each for the week.
◗Big night, zero riding after dark. Home by ~12:30 am by cab.

Fort, water sports, karting — then your own pool

Short riding day; the whole thing fits inside 12 km. Then the best-value night of the week: a fish market run, your own kitchen, your own pool, and nobody watching the clock.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
8:00 am
Breakfast at the villa
Slow one. On the road by 9:00 am — the Calangute–Candolim strip is the worst traffic in North Goa once the morning gets going.
9:30 – 11:15 am
Fort Aguada, Sinquerim◎— open in Google Maps
Free. Ramparts, the enormous freshwater cistern, and the four-storey lighthouse — the best-preserved Portuguese fort in Goa and the one that never fell. Best light on the laterite is around 10:00 am: high enough that the haze has burnt off, angled enough that the red stone still has texture. Shooting with the sun at 90° makes the sky go deep against it. Note the fort compound shuts well before sunset, so this is a morning stop, not the sunset stop your list assumed.
11:30 am – 1:00 pm
Sinquerim — water sports and the dolphin boat◎— open in Google Maps season-dependent
Sinquerim jetty is the calmest launch on this coast and where operators restart first. Dolphin trip ₹500–900 a head for an hour; parasailing ₹1,200–2,000; jet ski ~₹1,500; banana and bumper rides ₹500–800. Open-sea operators only run when the sea is declared safe, which in recent years has been from around mid-September. Ask at the jetty the day before rather than turning up hopeful. If it's running, this is the morning — by Thursday you've moved north and Sinquerim is 12 km away.
1:15 – 3:00 pm
Candolim — long lunch and the quiet sand◎— open in Google Maps
More upmarket and far less frantic than Baga, and the right place to burn the hottest part of the day. Feet up, nobody rushing.
3:30 – 4:30 pm
Track II Go-Karting, Arpora◎— open in Google Maps new
₹350 for 10 laps on a 340 m track; the pro karts run 13 hp Hondas. Twenty-five minutes, eight people, one timing board and a grudge. Cheapest fun on this page, and it's next door to where you sleep tomorrow.
4:45 pm
Fish market run — Calangute or Siolim◎— open in Google Maps for tonight
Kingfish or pomfret steaks, tiger prawns, crab if it looks good — ask them to clean and cut it. Pick up kokum, coconut milk, curry leaves, ginger-garlic and a packet of rechado masala on the way back. Buying fish at a market and cooking it yourself costs about a fifth of a shack dinner for eight. Go at 4:45 pm, not 6:00 pm — the good stuff is gone by evening.
5:45 pm
Sunset from the villa roof — 6:24 pm
Light until about 6:46 pm. Nothing to ride to today. Be back, and unhurried.
7:30 pm
Villa pool party and a Goan cook-up◎— open in Google Maps the best night, and the cheapest
Rechado-stuffed kingfish in the pan, prawn balchão, rice, sol kadhi, and the bebinca you bought on Tuesday. One speaker, the pool lit, and eight people who don't need a bar tab. Five nights out in a row flattens a group by Thursday — this is the night that stops it, and the only one where you set the volume, the music and the finish time. Split it before anyone opens a packet: two cook, two prep, two on ice and drinks, two on cleanup.
10:30 pm
LPK Waterfront, Nerul◎— open in Google Maps optional confirm
If the party still has legs: mud-and-stone architecture carved into the riverbank, a giant sculpted Buddha, deep house on a waterfront deck. The most striking-looking venue in Goa when it's running. A Wednesday in the last week of September is shoulder season — call and check their Instagram that week. SinQ in Candolim is the like-for-like swap if LPK is dark. And if you'd rather stay in the pool, stay in the pool; tomorrow is the big one.

◗Home is where you already are. Bed by ~1:00 am, later if the pool wins.

Move 1.5 km, then the cliff and the big night

Season opens today and the beaches switch on. Everything after the move sits inside a five-kilometre circle — Chapora, Ozran, Thalassa, Hammerzz — so this is the biggest day of the trip and the shortest ride home of it.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
8:30 am
Last villa breakfast — clear the kitchen out
Cook whatever's left. Anything unopened goes in a bag to the hostel, which has a common kitchen too.
10:00 am
Check out of Plot 77 — bags by taxi, riders by scooter◎— open in Google Maps
Plot 77 to The Hosteller Arpora is about 1.5 km, five minutes. Book one taxi or small tempo for the luggage and let the bikes run empty. Sweep every room and settle the villa deposit before anyone leaves. Eight people with eight bags on four scooters is unbalanced, illegal to load, and the most avoidable crash risk of the week. One ₹300 fare removes it entirely.
10:45 am
Drop bags — The Hosteller Goa, Arpora◎— open in Google Maps check-in time
Hostel check-in is usually early afternoon but luggage storage is normally available from the morning — confirm both when you book. There's a pool and a common kitchen here as well.
11:45 am – 1:45 pm
Baba Au Rhum, Anjuna◎— open in Google Maps closed one day
French-Italian café in bamboo groves overlooking open fields, under a kilometre from the new base. Shakshuka, the Nutella croissants, beef roast on ciabatta, Vietnamese iced coffee, guava smoothie. Check which weekday they close before you ride over.
2:15 – 3:30 pm
Chapora Fort◎— open in Google Maps
Free, unstaffed laterite ruin on a headland above the Chapora river mouth, looking across to Morjim. The Dil Chahta Hai wall is the one everyone comes for. The path up is bare laterite and genuinely slippery after rain — proper shoes, not flip-flops. Take the back path rather than the steep direct one if it's been wet.
3:45 – 4:45 pm
Ozran / Little Vagator and the red cliffs◎— open in Google Maps
The rock-carved Shiva face, red laterite cliffs, and the stepped path down to the cove. Big Vagator is the wide one you see from the fort; Ozran is the good one.
5:00 – 8:00 pm
Thalassa, Vagator◎— open in Google Maps reserve by message
Clifftop Greek taverna, whitewashed, west-facing, and the sunset table of North Goa. Sunset 6:23 pm, light until 6:45 pm, so the photo window is 5:50 – 6:25 pm. Greek dancing, plate-smashing and the fire show come later. Arrive at 5:00 pm for a perimeter table, not at 6:00 pm for whatever's left. Call +91 98500 33537, confirm which site you're booked at — they have operated more than one — and get it back in writing on WhatsApp. For the silhouette shot, lock exposure on the sky and let the group go black; metering on faces blows the horizon out completely.
9:30 pm
Hammerzz, Baga Rd◎— open in Google Maps the big one confirm
The proper big-room night: international-grade rig, serious lighting, and a separate hookah lounge upstairs that's easy to skip. 1.5 km from the hostel. This is the headline night, promoted from Friday because of the dry day. Thursday 1 October is season-opening night and the last night before a statewide dry day — exactly when Goan venues put an event on. Still call and check Instagram that week. Dress up; there's a door policy.

First light at Parra, the Friday bazaar, then north until the road runs out

Gandhi Jayanti — a statewide dry day, so no club, which frees the entire day for daylight. Your longest ride and your best beaches, starting before sunrise.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
6:45 – 7:45 am
Parra Road, the coconut avenue◎— open in Google Maps your friends asked for this
The palm-lined stretch running from Mapusa toward Parra village, six minutes from the hostel. Sunrise is 6:25 am. This only works before 8:00 am. At 7:00 am you get low light straight down the tunnel of palms, mist off the fields, and an empty road you can stand in the middle of. By 9:00 am it's a working road with buses and it photographs like any other road. The shot: one bike riding slowly away down the centre line, a second rider shooting low from behind.
8:15 – 9:45 am
Mapusa Friday Market◎— open in Google Maps moved to the right day
Runs year-round, but Friday is the bazaar and this is your only Friday. Stalls from 6:00 am; go between 8:00 am and 11:00 am before the produce sells out. Spices, cashews, Goan chorizo, bebinca, pickles, pottery, textiles. Mostly cash. This is where you buy the cashews and spices to take home — roughly a third of beach-stall prices and better quality. Buy from the women sitting on the floor rather than the shopfronts, haggle gently, and don't buy anything in the first ten minutes.
10:15 am – 12:00 pm
Artjuna or Café Antonio, and the Assagao lanes◎— open in Google Maps
Artjuna is a Portuguese-garden lifestyle space with a boutique and a library attached; the menu is Middle Eastern — falafel, hummus, cold-pressed juices. Expect 20–25 minutes for a table of eight. Café Antonio in Anjuna is the other option and is the photo spot on your list. Ride the Assagao lanes between the two — the prettiest five kilometres in North Goa.
12:45 – 2:15 pm
Morjim Beach◎— open in Google Maps
Wide estuary sand where the Chapora river meets the sea, and quiet even now. Getting here means the Siolim bridge crossing — add 20–25 minutes over what the map distance suggests. Olive ridley nesting runs November to March, so this is a beach-and-birds stop, not a turtle stop. Nobody should be walking the dunes looking for nests.
2:30 – 3:45 pm
Ashwem Beach◎— open in Google Maps
Quieter white sand, casuarina shade, the boho end of the coast. Late lunch here if Morjim ran short — from yesterday the shacks are legally open, so more will be running than you saw on Monday.
4:00 – 6:45 pm
Mandrem — sandbar, creek and the sunset◎— open in Google Maps new
Northernmost point of the trip: a sandbar, a tidal creek, and a bamboo footbridge over it. Sunset 6:22 pm, light until about 6:44 pm. Mandrem beats the open beaches at sunset because the creek gives you reflections and a foreground. Arambol is fifteen minutes further north if the group still has legs — but that's an extra 30 minutes on the ride home in the dark at the end of your longest riding day. Decide at 4:00 pm, not at 6:00 pm.
7:00 pm
Ride back south — 25 km, ~50 min
Leave while there's still colour in the sky. Longest ride home of the week and the Siolim road is unlit — tight convoy, double the following distance, last bike sets the pace.
8:30 pm
Last dinner — Vinayak, Assagao◎— open in Google Maps replaces the club night
Goan thali and sol kadhi, open until 11:00 pm. Gunpowder in Assagao is the other option. Both serve food normally on a dry day — only alcohol stops. Every restaurant in Goa is open on 2 October, so don't spend the evening riding around hunting for a club that isn't going to open. If a venue does announce a dry-day event, take it — it's the one night of the year the whole floor is as sober as you are.

    Why the south breaks

    • 195 km round trip — about five hours in the saddle before you've looked at anything.
    • NH66 south of Panjim is trucks, not coast road. It isn't a nice ride.
    • Leave Palolem at 6:45 pm and the whole return is after dark, unlit, on monsoon-wet tarmac.
    • Only four of you are licensed, so there's no rotation — the same four ride every kilometre.
    • On bikes you realistically see Palolem or Cabo de Rama. Not Chandor, not both.

    What the plan does instead

    • Day 1 — nothing hired. Baga is walkable and you've just travelled. ₹0.
    • Day 2 — one van, one driver for the far south, ~₹750–1,000 each, and it brings your groceries home.
    • Days 3–7 — scooters, 4½ days, everything inside a 30 km radius.
    • If you'd rather ride south anyway: leave at 6:00 am, cut Chandor, and start back by 4:00 pm so you're home before dark. You lose the sunset and a mansion.

    Everything else is scooter country

    • Five of your seven days never leave a 30 km radius.
    • Divar Island and the Assagao lanes are better on two wheels — that's the point of having them.
    • Parking in Panjim, Fontainhas, Mapusa and on Tito's Lane is trivial on a scooter and miserable in a car.
    • 4 bikes × ~₹500 × 4½ days ≈ ₹9,000, about ₹1,125 each. Fuel maybe ₹250 each on top.

    If a venue turns out to be shut

    • SinQ, Candolim — poolside cabanas, glass dance floor. Straight swap for LPK, four minutes away.
    • Chapora village lane — the old bar strip, alive after 10:30 pm. More crowd than club, and free to walk.
    • Curlies / Shiva Valley, Anjuna — trance on the sand, event-led. Check Instagram that week.
    • Casino Pride — the cheaper Mandovi boat if Deltin is full. Same 2 Oct closure.

    Making a club night work sober

    • Take a table. It costs about what eight entries cost anyway and gives you a base.
    • Order real cocktails minus the alcohol — virgin mojito, Shirley Temple, passionfruit sour. Nobody blinks.
    • Go at 10:30–11:00 pm, not 9:00 pm. Floors don't fill before 11:30 pm and you'll write the place off unfairly.
    • Agree the leaving time before you go in. Sober groups leave later, not earlier — nobody's flagging.
    • Pool for a cab on the two nights that are more than 5 km away. It's the cheapest safety you'll buy.
    Eating

    The kitchen, and where to eat when you're not using it

    You have a villa kitchen for four nights and a hostel kitchen after that. Used properly it pays for the scooters.

    One shop, four breakfasts

    • Newton's, Candolim — Monday 9:15 pm, in the van, on the way back from Palolem. No taxi, no carrying it on a bike.
    • Buy: eggs (4 dozen), poee and bread, coffee, tea, milk, curd, butter, jam, cereal, bananas, papaya, onions, tomatoes, chilli, oil, 2× 20 l water cans, snacks.
    • Rotation: omelette + poee, bhurji, French toast, fruit + curd. Two cook, two clean, rotate daily.
    • About ₹400 a morning for eight against ₹2,000 out — and an hour earlier on the road.

    The big cook-up — Wed 30

    • Calangute or Siolim fish market, 4:45 pm. Kingfish or pomfret steaks, tiger prawns, crab if it looks good. Ask them to clean it.
    • Rechado kingfish — slit the steaks, stuff with rechado masala, shallow fry in semolina. Fifteen minutes, hard to get wrong.
    • Prawn balchão and plain rice. Sol kadhi from kokum and coconut milk — pink, cold, best thing you'll drink all week.
    • Finish with the bebinca from Ribandar.

    Eat at least once

    • Fish thali — the default Goan lunch. Ritz Classic or Vinayak.
    • Chicken cafreal, pork vindaloo, sorpotel, chicken xacuti — the four you'd regret missing.
    • Poee with everything. Bebinca and serradura for dessert.
    • Sol kadhi after every heavy meal. It's a digestive, and it's why nobody feels wrecked at 4:00 pm.

    Contingency

    If it rains, and how to ride

    Late September is the tail of the southwest monsoon. Assume at least two wet days and agree the swaps now, rather than negotiating in a downpour with helmets on.

    Rain swaps, agreed in advance

    • Baga afternoon → Panjim: Goa State Museum, then Fontainhas on foot.
    • Palolem half of Monday → keep Chandor and Cabo de Rama, add Sahakari Spice Farm on the way back. You're in a van anyway.
    • Candolim beach → Aguada ramparts in the rain, honestly better, then a three-hour lunch.
    • Chapora climb → skip it. Wet laterite is the one genuinely dangerous surface here.
    • Far-north beaches → Assagao and Siolim cafés: short hops, all indoors, no highway.
    • Parra Road → pointless in flat grey light. Go straight to Mapusa at 8:00 am.
    • A washed-out day → Mayem bungee + Arvalem falls. Inland, and rain improves both.

    Before you take the keys

    • Video all four scooters, all four sides, before paying anything.
    • Eight helmets, not four. Goa enforces pillion helmets and North Goa police check.
    • Photograph each RC book and insurance; keep them on your phone.
    • Never hand over an original licence or passport as deposit — photocopy plus cash.
    • Check tyre tread and brakes yourself. Monsoon roads punish bald tyres.
    • Agree the fuel-return level and photograph the gauge.

    Convoy rules, with no leader

    • Only the four licensed riders ride. No swapping "just for a bit".
    • No trip leader here, so name a lead and a sweep out loud before every ride, and rotate it. With eight equals, everyone assumes someone else is watching the mirrors.
    • Four bikes leave together and arrive together. Nobody rides alone at night.
    • Last bike sets the pace; front bike checks mirrors at every turn.
    • Live location in one group chat on every night ride.
    • Cows on unlit roads after dark are the classic North Goa crash. Ride to your headlight.
    • No riding in active heavy rain — pull in, order coffee, wait. Goan downpours are short.
    • Painted markings, manhole covers and diesel patches at junctions are the hazard, not the rain.
