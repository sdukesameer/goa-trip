Four days north,

Sunday 27 September to Saturday 3 October 2026. Scooters for the four northern days, a tempo traveller for the two southern ones. Villa doors open at 11:00 am on the 27th, checkout is 11:00 am on the 3rd, and everything between is built around what is actually open that week — because three of the things on everyone's list are not.

Read first

Five things the calendar decides for you

You arrive in the last week of the monsoon, days before the season opens on 1 October. Most of the plan is unaffected. These are not — and three of them are on the list the group already put together.

Changes the last night

2 October is a statewide dry day

Gandhi Jayanti is Goa's one fixed dry day. Every casino is ordered shut for the full 24 hours and no alcohol is served anywhere — clubs, bars, shacks, shops.

You don't drink, so the ban itself costs you nothing. The catch is second-order: clubs don't open a room they can't run a bar in. So Friday isn't a club night, and the big night moves to Thursday 1 October — which is season-opening night anyway.

Headline club night Fri 2 Oct → Thu 1 Oct, Hammerzz
Cut it

Anjuna flea market won't be up on 30 September

30 Sep 2026 is a Wednesday, so the day is right — but the market is an October-to-April operation, shut through the monsoon. You miss it by one week.

The replacement is better and falls on a day you're already free: Mapusa Friday Market on 2 October — year-round, Friday is its bazaar day, ten minutes from Arpora, and it's where Goans actually shop.

Wed 30 Sep · Anjuna flea → Fri 2 Oct · Mapusa, 8:15 am
Cut it

Dudhsagar is closed, all of it

The Forest Department suspends the Collem jeep safari every monsoon and reopens around mid-to-late October, after your dates. The railway-track walk is banned and patrolled, with fines.

If someone needs a waterfall: Arvalem near Sanquelim is ~35 km from Baga, free, open, and at full flow right now — and it's ten minutes from the Mayem Lake bungee, so the two make one good half-day.

In your favour

Season starts 1 October, mid-trip

Shack licences run 1 Oct to 31 May, so the coast changes character halfway through your week: empty sand and red flags for the first four days, then everything switches on.

That's why this order exists — quiet beaches first (Palolem, Candolim, Sinquerim), switched-on ones after the 1st (Vagator, Morjim, Mandrem).

Your question, answered

Two south days earn their place — but only split this way

Doing the same 195 km run twice would not be worth it. Split by region, it is: Thursday is Canacona (Cabo de Rama, Agonda, Palolem — 195 km round trip) and Friday is Salcete (Mapusa bazaar, Chandor, Rachol, Loutolim, Colva and Betalbatim — 135 km). Different roads, different Goa, and Friday is half the driving.

If you'd rather save roughly ₹6,000 and a long day, cut Friday's van and keep 2 October in the north — Thursday alone still gets you every South Goa postcard. That's the only part of this week that is genuinely optional.

Yes

Central Goa comes free with the north

Panjim is 15 km from Baga and Old Goa 25 km — both comfortable scooter runs from where you're sleeping. So Central isn't a third region needing its own day; it's Monday, on the bikes, and it's the best wet-weather day you have.

Which means the four "north" days actually cover north and central, and the van days are pure south.

Keys, wheels, and the fort at sunset

You land from eight directions, pick up four scooters, and get the best sunset in North Goa on your first evening. Nothing today is worth being upset about losing.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
11:00 am⁙
Check in — Plot 77 by HNH, Baga◎— open in Google Maps
Drop bags, claim beds, photograph the villa's existing damage and the meter readings before anyone unpacks. You're arriving separately, so agree one arrival window in the group chat and one person who is definitely there at 11:00 am to take the keys. The worst version of today is three people sitting on bags outside a locked gate.
12:00 pm⁙
4 scooters delivered to the villa◎— open in Google Maps book ahead
Most Baga vendors will drop bikes at your door — ask when you book. ₹400–700 per bike per day. Activa or Jupiter over an NTorq if anyone is a nervous rider. Video all four bikes, all four sides, before money changes hands: scratches, mirrors, tyre tread, fuel gauge, horn, indicators. Count eight helmets, not four — Goa enforces pillion helmets. Photograph each RC and insurance. Never leave an original licence or passport as deposit; photocopy plus cash.
1:00 pm⁙
Lunch — Souza Lobo or Britto's◎— open in Google Maps
Two institutions on the sand, both older than the strip around them. Butter-garlic prawns and a fish thali shared out is the fastest way to learn what everyone eats.
2:30 pm⁙
Baga Beach and the river mouth◎— open in Google Maps red flags likely
Walk north to where the Baga river meets the sea — the rocks and moored boats are the photograph, not the crowded middle. Water sports restart when the sea is declared safe, usually mid-September. Assume a swimming ban for the first few days. Drishti lifeguards aren't negotiating: red flag means a beach day, not a swim day.
4:15 – 5:30 pm⁙
Fort Aguada, Sinquerim◎— open in Google Maps
Free. Ramparts, the enormous freshwater cistern, and the four-storey lighthouse — the best-preserved Portuguese fort in Goa, and the one that never fell. 6 km from the villa. The fort compound shuts before the sun goes down, so do the walls now and take the sunset from the beach below. That's the same horizon without a guard moving you on.
5:30 – 6:50 pm⁙
Sunset, Sinquerim Beach — 6:27 pm◎— open in Google Maps
Light until about 6:49 pm. The headland with the fort wall running down into the water is the shot — get the group on the sand with the fort behind and shoot into the sun, backlit, not squinting at it.
7:30 pm⁙
Stock the kitchen — Newton's, Candolim◎— open in Google Maps four breakfasts
Biggest supermarket in North Goa and directly on the way home. Eggs, poee and bread, coffee, tea, milk, curd, butter, jam, cereal, fruit, onions, chilli, oil, 2× 20 l water cans, snacks. Eight people eating breakfast out is ~₹2,000 a morning and an hour gone each time. A stocked kitchen makes it ~₹400 and gets you on the road by 8:15 am, which is the only thing standing between you and the good light at Fontainhas tomorrow.
9:30 pm⁙
Tito's Lane — Tito's and Mambo's◎— open in Google Maps confirm
The oldest strip in Goan nightlife, 1.7 km away, Bollywood and commercial EDM. Pool the money and take a table — the mocktail list is long and nobody cares what's in your glass. Sunday of a shoulder-season week: call to check they're running and whether a table minimum applies. Walk or take one cab. Tito's Lane parking at midnight is its own ordeal and it's a fifteen-minute walk.

Mon 28 Sep 🛵 scooters

The Latin Quarter, the churches, the river

Central Goa is a scooter day from Baga — Panjim is 15 km, Old Goa 25. Longest ride of the week and the best one for rain, since almost all of it works wet. Tonight is a casino boat at the weekday rate.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
7:30 am⁙
Breakfast at the villa
Eggs, poee, coffee, out the door by 8:15 am. Sunrise is 6:25 am — this start is what buys you empty lanes.
9:00 – 10:45 am⁙
Fontainhas, Panjim◎— open in Google Maps best light of the week
Asia's largest Latin Quarter — ochre, indigo and oxblood houses on non-motorable cobbled lanes. Park once at the Tobacco Square end and walk. The Chapel of St. Sebastian (the crucifix has Christ's eyes open — it came from the Palace of the Inquisition), Gallery Gitanjali, then Confeitaria 31 de Janeiro for a Swiss roll and a second coffee. These lanes act like canyons and fall into hard shadow by noon. Between 8:30 am and 10:00 am you get soft light off the pastel walls, no tour groups, and residents who don't mind you yet. Most time-sensitive stop of the week — late costs you the photographs, not the place.
11:05 am⁙
Fabs Bebinca, Ribandar◎— open in Google Maps
Fatima bakes the state's best bebinca — sixteen layers of coconut milk, egg yolk and nutmeg — out of her own heritage home. Not signposted; ask near the Ribandar church. Buy a whole one for the villa.
12:00 pm⁙
Basilica of Bom Jesus + Sé Cathedral◎— open in Google Maps
UNESCO-listed, both free, five minutes apart. St Francis Xavier's remains are in the Basilica; Sé is the largest church in Asia and holds the Golden Bell. Shoulders and knees covered. The Basilica closes to visitors during Mass — check times on arrival and do Sé first if you've walked into one. Both interiors are dim: brace against a pillar rather than pushing ISO, and shoot the Basilica facade from the south lawn, not head-on.
1:30 pm⁙
Lunch — Viva Panjim or Ritz Classic◎— open in Google Maps
Viva Panjim is a tiny Fontainhas house doing Goan home food; Ritz Classic is the fish-thali institution. Prawn balchão, chicken cafreal, kingfish rechado, sol kadhi.
2:45 – 5:15 pm⁙
Divar Island, by free ferry◎— open in Google Maps free crossing
The flat-bottom ferry leaves the Old Goa terminal by the Viceroy's Arch, runs roughly 6:00 am to midnight, and is free for two-wheelers. The far side has no traffic and no tourists — empty lanes through paddy and palm, and the hilltop church at Piedade looking back over the river. Best pure riding of the trip and the reason the scooters are worth having. Ask at the jetty what time the last crossing back runs and don't be on the island without knowing that number.
7:30 pm⁙
Deltin Royale casino boat, Panjim◎— open in Google Maps book
India's largest floating casino, boarding at Fisheries Jetty. Monday sits in the Mon–Thu band, so entry is ~₹2,500–3,000 and includes an unlimited buffet plus bonus gaming chips. Live entertainment from 7:00 pm. The big night that needs no alcohol at all — the buffet, the tables, the show, the boat. Weekends run ₹4,000+. Smart casuals, carry photo ID. Take two cabs and leave the bikes at the villa — Panjim to Baga at half past midnight is 16 km and not a ride to end this day on.

3Vagator
Tue 29 Sep 🛵 scooters
⁙

Anjuna, Chapora, the red cliffs, and the sunset table

The postcard day. Everything sits inside a ten-kilometre arc north of the villa, so it's the shortest riding and the most photographed — ending on the Thalassa cliff with the sun going down.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
9:30 – 11:15 am⁙
Baba Au Rhum, Anjuna◎— open in Google Maps closed one day
French-Italian café in bamboo groves over open fields. Shakshuka, the Nutella croissants, beef roast on ciabatta, Vietnamese iced coffee. Check which weekday they close before you ride over. Artjuna is the alternative and the better garden — a Portuguese courtyard with a boutique and a library attached, Middle Eastern menu, 20–25 minutes for a table of eight. Pick one; you cannot do both and still make Chapora.
11:30 am – 1:00 pm⁙
Anjuna Beach and Café Antonio◎— open in Google Maps photo spot
The rocky-cove end of Anjuna with the old Portuguese chapel above it. Café Antonio on the cliff is the photo spot on your friends' list — go for the terrace, not the menu.
1:15 pm⁙
Lunch — Anjuna or Assagao lanes◎— open in Google Maps
Artjuna, Gunpowder or Burger Factory. Ride the Assagao lanes between them — the prettiest five kilometres in North Goa and worth twenty minutes of going nowhere.
2:45 – 4:00 pm⁙
Chapora Fort◎— open in Google Maps
Free, unstaffed laterite ruin on the headland above the Chapora river mouth, looking across to Morjim. The Dil Chahta Hai wall is the one everyone comes for. The path up is bare laterite and genuinely slippery after rain — proper shoes, not flip-flops. Take the back path rather than the steep direct one if it's been wet.
4:15 – 5:00 pm⁙
Ozran / Little Vagator and the red cliffs◎— open in Google Maps
The rock-carved Shiva face, the red laterite cliffs, and the stepped path down to the cove. Big Vagator is the wide one you see from the fort; Ozran is the good one.
5:00 – 8:00 pm⁙
Thalassa, Vagator◎— open in Google Maps reserve by message
Clifftop Greek taverna, whitewashed, west-facing, the sunset table of North Goa. Sunset 6:25 pm, light to 6:47 pm, so the photo window is 5:50 – 6:27 pm. Greek dancing, plate-smashing and the fire show come later. Arrive at 5:00 pm for a perimeter table, not 6:00 pm for whatever's left. Call +91 98500 33537, confirm which site you're booked at — they have run more than one — and get it in writing on WhatsApp. For the silhouette, lock exposure on the sky and let the group go black; metering on faces blows the horizon out.
9:30 pm⁙
Hammerzz or LPK Waterfront◎— open in Google Maps confirm
Hammerzz on Baga Road is the big room — international-grade rig, serious lighting, 4 km from the villa. LPK at Nerul is the better-looking venue: mud-and-stone walls carved into the riverbank, a giant Buddha, deep house on a waterfront deck. This is now the headline club night, because the last two evenings are South Goa days and 2 October is a dry day. Call both and check Instagram the same week — shoulder-season venues announce there, not on a website. SinQ in Candolim is the third option.

4Far north
Wed 30 Sep 🛵 scooters
⁙

First light at Parra, the far north, then your own pool

Start before sunrise, ride to the top of the coast, come back down through the fish market, and spend the last villa night cooking in it. Longest ride of the week and the cheapest night of it.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
6:45 – 7:45 am⁙
Parra Road, the coconut avenue◎— open in Google Maps your friends asked for this
The palm-lined stretch from Mapusa toward Parra village, 8 km from the villa. Sunrise 6:25 am. This only works before 8:00 am. At 7:00 am you get low light straight down the tunnel of palms, mist off the fields, and an empty road you can stand in the middle of. By 9:00 am it's a working road with buses and photographs like any other road. The shot: one bike riding slowly away down the centre line, a second rider low and behind.
8:15 – 9:30 am⁙
Breakfast, Assagao◎— open in Google Maps
Vinayak for a Goan breakfast or Gunpowder's garden. You're 15 minutes from Parra and pointed north.
10:15 am – 12:00 pm⁙
Morjim Beach◎— open in Google Maps
Wide estuary sand where the Chapora river meets the sea, quiet even now. Getting here means the Siolim bridge — add 20–25 minutes over what the map distance suggests. Olive ridley nesting runs November to March, so this is a beach-and-birds stop, not a turtle stop.
12:15 – 2:00 pm⁙
Ashwem Beach and lunch◎— open in Google Maps
Quieter white sand, casuarina shade, the boho end of the coast. Lunch at one of the beach kitchens.
2:30 – 4:00 pm⁙
Arambol, if the group still has legs◎— open in Google Maps optional
The furthest north you can reasonably get — the sweetwater lake behind the beach and the banyan walk. 32 km from the villa, and the only stop today you can drop without regret. Decide at 2:30 pm, not at 5:00 pm. Arambol adds about 30 minutes each way onto the end of your longest riding day, and you want to be back at the coast for sunset, not still riding toward it.
4:30 – 6:50 pm⁙
Mandrem — sandbar, creek, sunset 6:24 pm◎— open in Google Maps best sunset
A sandbar, a tidal creek, and a bamboo footbridge over it. Light until about 6:46 pm. Mandrem beats every open beach at sunset because the creek gives you reflections and a foreground. Shoot from the footbridge looking west with the group on the sandbar.
7:15 pm⁙
Fish market, Siolim — on the way home◎— open in Google Maps for tonight
Kingfish or pomfret steaks, tiger prawns, crab if it looks good — ask them to clean and cut it. Grab kokum, coconut milk, curry leaves and a packet of rechado masala. Siolim sits exactly on the road back from Mandrem, which is why the fish run is today and not on a day you have to make a special trip for it.
8:30 pm⁙
Villa pool party and a Goan cook-up◎— open in Google Maps the best night, and the cheapest
Rechado-stuffed kingfish in the pan, prawn balchão, rice, sol kadhi, and the bebinca from Monday. One speaker, the pool lit, eight people who don't need a bar tab. Last night with a kitchen and a pool — from tomorrow you're in a hostel and then in a van for two days. Split the jobs before anyone opens a packet: two cook, two prep, two on ice, two on cleanup.

South Goa, day one — the far end

Scooters go back, the van arrives, and it moves your luggage to Arpora on the way out of town. Then Canacona: the cliff fort, Agonda, and Palolem. The longest drive of the trip, done sitting down.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
7:00 am⁙
Breakfast, pack, return the scooters◎— open in Google Maps
Vendor collects from the villa. Refuel to the level you took them at, photograph the gauge, play back the day-one videos before anyone discusses the deposit, and get the cash back on the spot. Do this before the van arrives, not while it's waiting. Every scooter-deposit argument in Goa starts with someone in a hurry.
8:00 am⁙
Van picks up the group and the luggage◎— open in Google Maps book ahead
One 12-seater Tempo Traveller with a driver for both South days. Agree the stop list and the price in writing the night before. Booking the same van and driver for Thursday and Friday is worth a discount and means one phone number instead of two. An open-ended 'South Goa sightseeing' booking is how you end up at a shop that pays the driver a commission.
8:20 am⁙
Drop bags — The Hosteller Goa, Arpora◎— open in Google Maps check-in time
1.5 km from the villa and directly on the way out. Hostel check-in is usually early afternoon but luggage storage is normally available from the morning — confirm both when you book. This is the whole reason the van day and the moving day are the same day. Eight people with eight bags on four scooters was the single most avoidable crash risk of the week, and now it doesn't exist.
11:00 am – 12:15 pm⁙
Cabo de Rama Fort◎— open in Google Maps
Open 9:00 am–5:00 pm, free. Laterite ramparts, old cannons, the Chapel of St Anthony, and cliff viewpoints dropping straight into the Arabian Sea. The best photograph in South Goa is the cliff edge past the chapel. In late September the grass is waist-high and violently green — but the drop is unfenced and wet laterite is slick. Stay back from the edge and nobody walks to the point alone.
12:45 – 2:00 pm⁙
Lunch and a walk — Agonda Beach◎— open in Google Maps
Two and a half kilometres of near-empty sand. Calmer than Palolem, a better lunch this early in the season, and the one stop today where nobody needs to be doing anything.
2:30 – 6:50 pm⁙
Palolem Beach — sunset 6:23 pm◎— open in Google Maps pre-season
The crescent everyone photographs. Walk it end to end, take the rocks at the south end toward Colomb, and wait for the light. Usable until about 6:45 pm. Set expectations: hut licences start 1 October, so the beach is literally being rebuilt today — scaffolding, few kitchens open, red flags, no swimming, and no boats to Butterfly or Honeymoon beach. What you get instead is the most famous bay in Goa with almost nobody on it. You cannot take that photograph in December.
6:50 pm⁙
North, and eat on the road
Two and a half hours back. Stop at Margao rather than arriving in Arpora at 10:00 pm hunting for a kitchen.

6Mid south
Fri 2 Oct 🚐 tempo traveller
⁙

South Goa, day two — the bazaar and the mansions

A completely different south: Friday's bazaar first, then Salcete's Portuguese heartland and the emptiest white sand in the state. Half the driving of yesterday and none of the same road.

▸ Open this day's route in Google Maps
7:45 – 9:00 am⁙
Mapusa Friday Market◎— open in Google Maps your only Friday
The van collects you and stops here first — Mapusa is ten minutes from Arpora. Runs year-round, but Friday is the bazaar. Spices, cashews, Goan chorizo, bebinca, pickles, pottery, textiles. Mostly cash. This is where you buy the cashews and spices to take home — about a third of beach-stall prices and better quality. Buy from the women sitting on the floor rather than the shopfronts, and don't buy anything in the first ten minutes. The van holds your shopping all day, which is the other reason it's today.
10:30 am – 12:00 pm⁙
Braganza House, Chandor◎— open in Google Maps phone first
A 350-year-old Portuguese mansion still lived in by the family — ballroom, Belgian chandeliers, a long rosewood gallery, and a relic of St Francis Xavier in the private chapel. Two wings entered separately: ~₹150 a head one side, a donation box the other. Open most days, roughly 9:00/10:00 am to 5:00 pm. It's a home, not a museum, and eight unannounced visitors is a lot — ring ahead. Carry cash, there's no card machine. Photography rules differ between the wings, so ask. Mid-morning is when light rakes down the long gallery, which is exactly when you're there.
12:20 – 1:00 pm⁙
Rachol Seminary◎— open in Google Maps new
A 16th-century seminary and a museum of Christian art that almost nobody visits — painted ceilings, a working pipe organ, and a fortified gate. Fifteen minutes from Chandor. Call ahead or accept you may only see the church and the courtyard; the museum keeps its own hours and it is still a functioning seminary. Even the outside is worth the stop.
1:15 – 2:30 pm⁙
Loutolim — Figueiredo Mansion and Mario Miranda's village◎— open in Google Maps new
A 400-year-old Portuguese-era house with its own library and chapel, in the village the cartoonist Mario Miranda came from. Ancestral Goa next door is a walk-through of old village life. Lunch here or in Margao at Longuinhos. Two heritage houses in one day is the right number — three is a slog. If the group is flagging, skip Figueiredo and just walk the village; the lanes and the church square are the point.
3:15 – 5:00 pm⁙
Colva Beach◎— open in Google Maps
The oldest and best-known beach in South Goa, and the busiest thing you'll see all day — which after yesterday is a change of pace rather than a problem.
5:00 – 6:50 pm⁙
Betalbatim and Majorda — sunset 6:22 pm◎— open in Google Maps the quiet one
Ten minutes north of Colva and almost deserted: golden sand, casuarina groves, and Sunset Point at Betalbatim. Light until about 6:44 pm. This is the best-kept stretch in Goa and the reason the second South day earns its place. Walk north from Betalbatim into Majorda as the light goes — nobody on it, and the palms come right down to the sand.
8:30 pm⁙
Last dinner, Assagao or Anjuna◎— open in Google Maps dry day
Vinayak for a thali and sol kadhi, or Gunpowder. Every restaurant in Goa serves food normally on 2 October; only alcohol stops. Don't spend the evening hunting for a club that isn't going to open. If a venue does announce a dry-day event, take it — it's the one night of the year the whole floor is as sober as you are.

    Why the south breaks

    • 195 km round trip — about five hours in the saddle before you've looked at anything.
    • NH66 south of Panjim is trucks, not coast road. It isn't a nice ride.
    • Leave Palolem at 6:45 pm and the whole return is after dark, unlit, on monsoon-wet tarmac.
    • Only four of you are licensed, so there's no rotation — the same four ride every kilometre.
    • On bikes you realistically see Palolem or Cabo de Rama. Not Chandor, not both.

    What the plan does instead

    • Day 1 — nothing hired. Baga is walkable and you've just travelled. ₹0.
    • Day 2 — one van, one driver for the far south, ~₹750–1,000 each, and it brings your groceries home.
    • Days 3–7 — scooters, 4½ days, everything inside a 30 km radius.
    • If you'd rather ride south anyway: leave at 6:00 am, cut Chandor, and start back by 4:00 pm so you're home before dark. You lose the sunset and a mansion.

    Everything else is scooter country

    • Five of your seven days never leave a 30 km radius.
    • Divar Island and the Assagao lanes are better on two wheels — that's the point of having them.
    • Parking in Panjim, Fontainhas, Mapusa and on Tito's Lane is trivial on a scooter and miserable in a car.
    • 4 bikes × ~₹500 × 4½ days ≈ ₹9,000, about ₹1,125 each. Fuel maybe ₹250 each on top.

    If a venue turns out to be shut

    • SinQ, Candolim — poolside cabanas, glass dance floor. Straight swap for LPK, four minutes away.

    Making a club night work sober

    • Take a table. It costs about what eight entries cost anyway and gives you a base.
    • Order real cocktails minus the alcohol — virgin mojito, Shirley Temple, passionfruit sour. Nobody blinks.
    • Go at 10:30–11:00 pm, not 9:00 pm. Floors don't fill before 11:30 pm and you'll write the place off unfairly.
    • Agree the leaving time before you go in. Sober groups leave later, not earlier — nobody's flagging.
    • Pool for a cab on the two nights that are more than 5 km away. It's the cheapest safety you'll buy.
    Eating

    The kitchen, and where to eat when you're not using it

    You have a villa kitchen for four nights and a hostel kitchen after that. Used properly it pays for the scooters.

    One shop, four breakfasts

    • Newton's, Candolim — Monday 9:15 pm, in the van, on the way back from Palolem. No taxi, no carrying it on a bike.
    • Buy: eggs (4 dozen), poee and bread, coffee, tea, milk, curd, butter, jam, cereal, bananas, papaya, onions, tomatoes, chilli, oil, 2× 20 l water cans, snacks.
    • Rotation: omelette + poee, bhurji, French toast, fruit + curd. Two cook, two clean, rotate daily.
    • About ₹400 a morning for eight against ₹2,000 out — and an hour earlier on the road.

    The big cook-up — Wed 30

    • Calangute or Siolim fish market, 4:45 pm. Kingfish or pomfret steaks, tiger prawns, crab if it looks good. Ask them to clean it.
    • Rechado kingfish — slit the steaks, stuff with rechado masala, shallow fry in semolina. Fifteen minutes, hard to get wrong.
    • Prawn balchão and plain rice. Sol kadhi from kokum and coconut milk — pink, cold, best thing you'll drink all week.
    • Finish with the bebinca from Ribandar.

    Eat at least once

    • Fish thali — the default Goan lunch. Ritz Classic or Vinayak.
    • Chicken cafreal, pork vindaloo, sorpotel, chicken xacuti — the four you'd regret missing.
    • Poee with everything. Bebinca and serradura for dessert.
    • Sol kadhi after every heavy meal. It's a digestive, and it's why nobody feels wrecked at 4:00 pm.

    Contingency

    If it rains, and how to ride

    Late September is the tail of the southwest monsoon. Assume at least two wet days and agree the swaps now, rather than negotiating in a downpour with helmets on.

    Rain swaps, agreed in advance

    • Baga afternoon → Panjim: Goa State Museum, then Fontainhas on foot.
    • Palolem half of Monday → keep Chandor and Cabo de Rama, add Sahakari Spice Farm on the way back. You're in a van anyway.
    • Candolim beach → Aguada ramparts in the rain, honestly better, then a three-hour lunch.
    • Chapora climb → skip it. Wet laterite is the one genuinely dangerous surface here.
    • Far-north beaches → Assagao and Siolim cafés: short hops, all indoors, no highway.
    • Parra Road → pointless in flat grey light. Go straight to Mapusa at 8:00 am.
    • A washed-out day → Mayem bungee + Arvalem falls. Inland, and rain improves both.

    Before you take the keys

    • Video all four scooters, all four sides, before paying anything.
    • Eight helmets, not four. Goa enforces pillion helmets and North Goa police check.
    • Photograph each RC book and insurance; keep them on your phone.
    • Never hand over an original licence or passport as deposit — photocopy plus cash.
    • Check tyre tread and brakes yourself. Monsoon roads punish bald tyres.
    • Agree the fuel-return level and photograph the gauge.

    Convoy rules, with no leader

    • Only the four licensed riders ride. No swapping "just for a bit".
    • No trip leader here, so name a lead and a sweep out loud before every ride, and rotate it. With eight equals, everyone assumes someone else is watching the mirrors.
    • Four bikes leave together and arrive together. Nobody rides alone at night.
    • Last bike sets the pace; front bike checks mirrors at every turn.
    • Live location in one group chat on every night ride.
    • Cows on unlit roads after dark are the classic North Goa crash. Ride to your headlight.
    • No riding in active heavy rain — pull in, order coffee, wait. Goan downpours are short.
    • Painted markings, manhole covers and diesel patches at junctions are the hazard, not the rain.
